The general method:
  1. If fragments are composition-only (not ordered), first work out each fragment's internal order using cut-site rules. Example: a Trypsin fragment must end in Arg or Lys, since that's the residue Trypsin cuts after, unless it's the very last fragment in the chain.
  2. Scan for overlaps between different enzymes' fragments. A fragment boundary from one enzyme often falls in the middle of another enzyme's fragment. That overlap is real evidence those residues are adjacent.
  3. Chain fragments together using those overlaps, extending outward from a fixed anchor (the N-terminus if revealed, or the fragment you're most sure of).
  4. Verify: every fragment from every revealed enzyme must appear as an unbroken stretch in your final sequence, with nothing missing or duplicated.
